Why 3×10 stops working

Your body adapts to what you repeat. Same weight, same reps every week, and it stops having a reason to change. Without data you can't tell whether you had more in you today, whether you were already spent, or whether you've been stuck for a month.

What POWR does differently

It measures the speed of every rep — the most direct window into real effort — and pushes you to change: add weight when the bar flies, stop when it slows too much, and see whether each lift is rising, holding or slipping. A coach that decides on real data.

How it measures

Your phone already has a lab inside.

Every modern phone carries an accelerometer and a gyroscope — the same kind of motion sensors that dedicated VBT devices use. Clipped to the bar, the phone moves exactly as the bar does. Here's how POWR turns that motion into numbers:

  1. 1 · It feels the motion

    The accelerometer measures the bar's acceleration about 100 times a second, and the gyroscope measures how it rotates. Studies with this type of sensor show 100 samples a second is enough, even at maximal loads.

  2. 2 · It removes gravity

    The phone always knows which way is down. POWR subtracts gravity and keeps only the bar's vertical movement.

  3. 3 · It computes speed — and cancels the error

    Integrating acceleration gives speed. Every sensor's problem is that tiny errors add up (the red line). But at the top and bottom of every rep the bar stops — speed is certainly zero there — and POWR uses those moments to wipe the error, rep after rep.

  4. 4 · It finds every rep

    It detects reps by the distance the bar travels, and computes five metrics: mean, peak and propulsive velocity, power and range of motion.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is the app free?

Yes. Tracking, coaching, history, progress and leaderboards are free. You only buy the kit once (€29) to hold your phone on the bar. Optional extras may come later, but the essentials stay free.

How can a phone be accurate?

Your phone has the same type of motion sensors as dedicated VBT devices. In a 2023 study, an Apple device strapped to the bar measured mean velocity with a typical error of 0.049 m/s against a 3D motion-capture lab — better than a €329 dedicated sensor. POWR uses the same method: remove gravity, filter the noise, and cancel the error every time the bar stops.

What is velocity-based training (VBT)?

Training that uses bar speed as the measure of effort. With the same weight, the bar moves faster on a good day than on a bad one. VBT measures that and adjusts your training to how you are today — not to what the plan said.

Which phones does it work with?

Any MagSafe iPhone (iPhone 12 or later) snaps on directly; other phones use the included magnetic ring, which sticks to your case. The app is currently in beta on iPhone, and every kit includes access.

Is it as accurate as a €500 sensor?

For what matters in training — seeing when you slow down and how you progress — it's useful and consistent. A lab linear encoder is still more accurate in absolute terms. That's why the POWR Collar will come later.

Will my phone fall off?

The clip grips the bar with rubber strips and the magnetic ring is double-sided N52. We test it for load, pull-off and heat. We still don't recommend it for Olympic lifts where the bar is dropped from overhead.

Do I need a subscription?

No. You buy the kit once and the app is free: tracking, history, progress and leaderboards.
